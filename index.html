Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about International Shipcare's vessel layup solutions.

What is the difference between hot and cold layup?

Hot Layup: The vessel remains partially operational with a reduced skeleton crew on board. Machinery and systems are kept running in an economical manner, allowing for immediate or rapid reactivation (within days). Typically reduces daily operating costs by 30%–50%.

Cold Layup: The vessel is fully decommissioned—crew signs off, major machinery is shut down, and the ship is put under structured preservation with 24/7 watchkeepers remaining. This option delivers maximum savings (80%–90% reduction in operating costs) with a targeted 14-day reactivation window.

Which layup option is suitable for my vessel?

Choose Hot Layup if your vessel will be idle short-term (under 4–6 months) or requires immediate return to service on short notice.

Choose Cold Layup if the vessel will remain inactive for extended periods (6–12+ months) and achieving maximum cost reduction is your primary goal.

What information is required for a quotation?

To prepare an accurate proposal, please provide:

  • Contact details (Name, Company, Phone, Email)
  • Vessel particulars (Name, Type, NRT, GRT, and DWT)
  • Layup parameters (Hot vs. Cold, estimated duration)
  • Additional service requirements (Underwater works, tank cleaning, logistics, bunkering, or repairs)
Which vessel layup locations are available?

ISC operates across strategic sheltered anchorages in Malaysia, including:

  • Brunei Bay (Sabah, Malaysia): Flagship location and the world's first officially gazetted lay-up anchorage (2008).
  • Labuan Anchorage (Labuan F.T., Malaysia)
  • Kemaman & Kuantan Ports (Peninsular Malaysia)
  • Other sheltered waters including Port Klang and Johor.
How is the vessel maintained during layup?

Vessels are protected using multi-layered preservation strategies:

  • Dehumidification: Deploying industrial dehumidifiers and sealing spaces to prevent corrosion.
  • Machinery Preservation: Applying corrosion inhibitors and periodically rotating machinery.
  • 24/7 Watchkeeping: Onboard watchkeepers supported by a land-based communications center and offshore patrol boats.
  • Hull & Underwater Care: Hull inspections (ROVs/drones), underwater cleaning, and cathodic protection.
What reports will vessel owners receive?

Owners receive structured reporting to verify condition and compliance:

  • Lay-Up Completion Report: Upon finishing initial setup and preservation.
  • Weekly Status Reports: Regular operational and security updates.
  • Monthly Technical Reports: In-depth technical evaluations, humidity logs, and machinery checks.
Can International Shipcare support vessel reactivation?

Yes. All preservation and maintenance programs are designed for safe and predictable recommissioning. ISC assists with machinery testing, coordinating Classification Society surveys, supply chain logistics, and fuel/bunker delivery to meet charter schedules.
